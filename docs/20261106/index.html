Executive overview

PoWatch: a webcam that turns a room into a story and a set of numbers

You point a camera at a room and press Start. The browser watches, listens and takes notes, and the server keeps only those notes, never the video. Later you get charts, a diary and a short story of what happened.

PoWatch is a hosted, single-user web app: a Blazor WebAssembly front end served by an ASP.NET Core API on .NET 10, with Azure Table and Blob Storage behind it. Every frame is analysed in the browser. The server ingests 10-second summaries ("ticks"), rolls them up into minute, hour, day and all-time totals, and serves statistics, recaps and stories.

Who it is for

One person who wants to know what goes on in a space while they are away, such as a room, a porch or a pet's corner, and who likes numbers.

The owner

Signs in with a Microsoft account (Entra ID). Everything they record lands in their own data partition; nobody else's data is visible to them.

Someone with a share link

Gets a frozen, read-only copy of one day's recap for 1, 7 or 30 days. They need no account, and the page runs no scripts.

Major features

Live

Camera in the middle and the room's numbers around it: who is in frame, motion heat-grid, light curve, "today vs your usual day", and a running narration (SAW · SAID · HEARD · IN · OUT).

Journal

Every day and session: a story first, then a minute-by-minute replay with a scrubber, the timeline of every line, moments with snapshots, and the weather.

Stories & recaps

A recap for each day and a story for each session (one chapter per ten minutes). Any AI may only reword these. The numbers always come from the template.

Ask PoWatch

A question in plain English ("when was the cat last here?") answered from your own statistics by an AI that may only use four lookup tools.

Regulars

Repeat visitors are recognised by their look (colour signature, never the face), and in the browser that learned it, by voice. You can name them.

Sharing, export, alerts

Share links, CSV/JSON/PDF downloads, watch rules ("a person between 22:00 and 06:00"), and an optional weekly email.

High-level snapshot

The browser does the seeing and hearing, the server does the counting and remembering, and an AI (when switched on) only polishes the wording.

  1. Watch locallyThe browser runs the vision, caption and audio models on your own machine. Frames never leave it.
  2. SummariseEvery 10 seconds it packs motion, light, sound level, object counts and notable events into one tick.
  3. Send safelyTicks go to the server in batches. If the network drops, they wait in the browser's outbox and are sent later.
  4. Count onceThe server files the raw rows and updates the running totals exactly once per batch.
  5. Tell the storyPages read the totals. A template writes the recap, and an AI may reword it but never change a number.
