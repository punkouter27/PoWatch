User journeys & experience

What people do with PoWatch, step by step

There are two main places: Live, where you watch the room right now, and Journal, where you read what already happened. Everything else (asking, sharing, deleting) hangs off those two.

Journey 1 · Sign in

Like showing ID at a building's front desk: Microsoft confirms who you are, and PoWatch gives you a visitor badge that lasts a day and renews while you are active.

  1. Open any pageThe app shell and its files load without a badge, so the sign-in page can appear.
  2. Choose MicrosoftYou are sent to Microsoft's sign-in page (OpenID Connect with PKCE).
  3. Come back with a badgeThe server checks which organisation you belong to, then sets an encrypted, HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie.
  4. Stay signed inThe badge lasts 24 hours and slides forward on each request, so a wall display never drops to the login page.

In Development and Test there is also "Continue as Guest" (/auth/login/fake). It is switched off in Production, and the server refuses to start if someone turns it on there.

Journey 2 · Record a session on Live

Press Start and walk away. The browser watches and listens, writes a line whenever something happens ("SAW a person walk across the room"), and sends a short note to the server every 10 seconds.

  1. StartPOST /api/sessions opens a session tagged with your time zone.
  2. Sense on-devicePixels four times a second, objects about once a second, captions when the scene changes, and sound level once a second.
  3. NarrateEach caption, line of speech, recognised sound, entrance and exit becomes a line under the camera.
  4. Batch & queueTicks are queued in IndexedDB (powatch-outbox) and posted to /api/sessions/{id}/batches.
  5. Live refreshThe server pushes statsChanged over SignalR, and the panels re-read their numbers.
  6. ChaptersEvery 10 minutes (plus 30 seconds so the last batches land), the browser asks for that stretch's chapter.
  7. StopThe final chapter is written, the chapters are joined into one story, and the recap card floats in.
Edge case: closing the tab. Leaving or reloading the page ends the recording. A session with no batches for 10 minutes is closed at its last activity the next time sessions are listed or read. Batches still in the outbox are sent later. If they are more than 5 minutes outside the session's window, the server rejects them.

Journey 3 · Read the Journal

The Journal is a diary that writes itself. Pick a day or a session. You read its story first, then scrub through a replay of every minute with the lines of what happened.

  1. Pick a dayThe index lists every day that has sessions, newest first, and nests the sessions inside.
  2. Read the recapThe template recap paints at once (?ai=false), then the AI wording replaces it if a model answers.
  3. Scrub the replayGET /api/stats/replay returns each observed minute with its 16×9 motion grid. Clicking a line jumps the playhead to it.
  4. Save"Text ↓" saves the lines as plain text (a data: link, no request). JSON and PDF come from the server.

Journey 4 · Ask a question

Type a question like "Was it busier than usual today?". An AI looks up the answer in your own numbers using four lookup tools and replies in one or two plain sentences.

  1. AskThe Ask box on Live posts to /api/ask.
  2. Look upThe model may call get_stats, get_day, get_patterns and search_captions, for at most four rounds.
  3. Answer or explainWithout a server AI, the box answers "503: no AI provider is configured" instead of guessing.

Journey 5 · Share one day

Like photocopying one diary page and handing it over: the copy is frozen, has no photos, and stops working after 1, 7 or 30 days, or as soon as you take it back.

  1. CreatePOST /api/shares freezes the day's recap without its moments and stores it under a 128-bit random token.
  2. Hand outAnyone with /s/{token} sees plain HTML: no scripts, not indexed, not cached.
  3. RevokeDELETE /api/shares/{token}, or "delete everything", ends it at once.

Journey 6 · Teach it who is who

PoWatch notices someone who keeps coming back and asks "who is this?". You give them a name, and from then on lines say "Sam came in". It recognises people by clothing colours, never by face.

  1. SpotThe tracker gives each person a colour signature, and /api/regulars/observe matches it to known regulars.
  2. NameThe bell asks you to "name this newcomer". PATCH /api/regulars/{id} saves the name, and earlier "came in" lines are rewritten.
  3. Voices (optional)When one person is alone in frame and speaks, the browser learns their voiceprint. It stays in this browser's localStorage and never goes to the server.

Journey 7 · Keep less, or delete everything

You decide how long the detailed notes are kept (30 days, 90 days, a year, or forever). "Delete everything" empties your drawer in the filing cabinet, including share links.

  1. Pick a windowPUT /api/profile with 30, 90 or 365 days. Every 6 hours a job prunes older ticks, events, snapshots and ledger claims. Totals and stories stay.
  2. Delete everythingDELETE /api/data purges every table, the snapshot photos, and share links found by owner. The browser also forgets its voiceprints.
